The Hero Rankings

Six very different characters. One very subjective scoring system. Let's see who deserves the top spot — and who might be overrated.

A word on the ranking system: scores are out of 10 on traits like Bravery, Loyalty, Crisis management, Diplomacy, Great entrances, Outfit game, and Daring deeds. You don't have to agree. Argue with us!
Rumi
K-Pop Demon Hunter · AKA The Star Slayer

Rumi lives a dual naturetwo very different sides to your personality. By day: K-pop star with millions of fans. By night: demon hunter.

But here's the twist: she's half-demon herself. Spoiler alertwarning: I'm about to reveal something important, that's a problem. If she gives in tostops resisting a feeling or impulse her darker instincts, she could lose herself completely.

Key idea → Powerful but conflicted. Loyal but isolated.

Sherlock Holmes
The Detective · AKA The Baker Street Brainbox

Sherlock doesn't have superpowers. He has a genius-level mind. He notices what others miss. He connects dots no one else sees — and he'd rather not be thanked afterwards.

But being the smartest person in the room has a cost. He can be cold, awkward, emotionally unavailablenot good at sharing or connecting emotionally. His friend Dr Watson helps him connect with actual humans. Sherlock cares about truth, not praise.

Key idea → Brilliant but difficult. Effective but lonely.
Bravery7/10
Loyalty6/10
Diplomacy5/10
Outfit game10/10
Total53/70
"You see, but you do not observe."
Harry Potter
The Wizard · AKA The Boy Who Lived

Harry's story starts with loss. But that pain shapes him. At Hogwarts, he finds friendship, purpose, and a fight worth joining. He's a classic hero — orphaned, unloved, but fiercely resilientable to bounce back from hard times.

He's not perfect. He gets angry. He makes mistakes. But when it matters, he shows up. He stands up tobravely opposes something unfair injustice — even at risk to himself. His outfit game, however, lets him down.

Key idea → Classic hero. Flawed but fearless.

"Expecto Patronum!"
Odysseus
The Strategist · AKA Iliad Lad

Odysseus isn't just strong — he's smart. He outwits monsters. He invents the Trojan Horse. He survives gods, storms, and his own mistakes. One of the earliest heroes in Greek mythology, he uses wit, not brute force.

But his biggest enemy is his ego. His hubrisexcessive pride that leads to problems costs him years, and hurts the people he loves. Brave and cunning, but arrogant.

Key idea → Cunning and courageous — but pride is his weakness.
Bravery10/10
Loyalty2/10
Diplomacy2/10

"My fame has reached the skies."
Elsa
The Ice Queen · AKA Queen of Arendelle

Elsa's power is beautiful — and dangerous. When she accidentally hurts her sister Anna, she does what she thinks is right: she isolates herself, choosing to hide away to keep Anna and her kingdom safe.

Her journey isn't about defeating a villain. It's about accepting herself — learning that love isn't about hiding, it's about being seen. (Real talk: Anna might be the actual hero here.)

Key idea → Powerful but fearful. Vulnerability is strength.

Loyalty10/10

"The cold never bothered me anyway."
Katniss Everdeen
The Survivor · AKA The Reluctant Hero

Katniss never asked to be a symbol. In The Hunger Games, a reluctant herosomeone who helps but doesn't want recognition emerges. When her sister is chosen, Katniss volunteers in her place — to save her, not for fame.

She's not polished. She's suspicious. She makes impulsive decisions. She hates being watched, praised, labelled "a hero." But when it counts, she acts. Not for glory. For people.

Key idea → Real, raw, resilient. A hero who doesn't want the title.
